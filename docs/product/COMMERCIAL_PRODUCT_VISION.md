# ArchaeoAI commercial product vision

## Status and boundary

This is a Phase 6A design hypothesis, not a deployed product, public inference authorization, or
claim of validated demand. Phase 5E remains incomplete, Phase 5F remains unauthorized, and the
scientific status remains `RQ1_PROVISIONALLY_ANSWERED_PENDING_REVIEW`.

## Problem

Professional land and heritage work can require substantial manual review of terrain and related
evidence. A carefully bounded tool may help qualified teams prioritize terrain morphology for
review while preserving provenance, uncertainty, and human accountability. ArchaeoAI has not yet
demonstrated time savings, cost savings, customer demand, or suitability for a live professional
decision.

## Proposed product

**A controlled archaeological-terrain screening workspace that processes authorized terrain,
records bounded AI-generated terrain-pattern hypotheses, supports qualified human review, and
produces auditable project reports without presenting machine output as archaeological fact.**

The smallest credible value proposition is review prioritization and traceability. The product
would help a professional organize a defined screening task; it would not make the final heritage,
planning, legal, or construction decision.

## Non-goals

- Automatic confirmation or rejection of archaeology.
- Replacement of archaeologists or professional judgement.
- Planning approval, legal heritage advice, or automatic construction clearance.
- England-wide archaeological certainty or transfer beyond the bounded E001 evidence.
- A public candidate map, location feed, or discovery engine.
- A calibrated “chance that archaeology exists” or an archaeology-free determination.
- Unsupervised scanning of land without lawful authority and a defined project purpose.

## Likely user groups—not confirmed customers

| Possible user | Job to be done | Information supplied | Needed output | Expertise still required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Archaeological consultancy | Prioritize terrain review within an authorized project area. | Project boundary, authorized DTM, project context, review policy. | Traceable screening queue and bounded report. | Qualified archaeological interpretation and follow-up design. |
| Environmental or heritage consultancy | Integrate terrain screening into a wider evidence assessment. | Authorized terrain, scope, constraints, existing desk-study context. | Auditable hypotheses and limitations for expert review. | Heritage assessment, source reconciliation, and professional sign-off. |
| Infrastructure project team | Commission a defined, reviewable terrain-screening task. | Project authority, data rights, area and purpose. | Status, provenance, review trail, and professional report. | Archaeological consultant oversight and statutory-process advice. |
| Renewable-energy project team | Identify terrain requiring early specialist attention. | Authorized project data and screening brief. | Prioritized expert-review workflow, not clearance. | Archaeologist assessment and planning/legal judgement. |
| Planning or land-development team | Support early evidence gathering without treating absence as clearance. | Authorized terrain, project scope, access permissions. | Bounded screening summary with unresolved risks. | Qualified heritage advice and formal decision-making. |
| Qualified archaeological professional | Review machine-generated terrain hypotheses consistently. | Review credentials, annotations, supporting evidence. | Separated AI/human evidence record and auditable report. | Domain interpretation, fieldwork recommendations, and accountable conclusions. |

These are discovery hypotheses for future interviews. They are not customers, endorsements, or
evidence of product-market fit.

## Product principles

1. **Human authority:** software can organize and score terrain patterns but cannot make an
   archaeological determination.
2. **Evidence separation:** AI output, AI hypothesis, human observation, archaeological
   interpretation, and confirmed evidence remain distinct records.
3. **Private by default:** locations and location-linked material remain within the authorized
   project boundary and are never public by default.
4. **Reuse, do not fork:** future product processing must call the verified Phase 5 contracts rather
   than create a second commercial feature path.
5. **Fail closed:** ambiguous inputs, artifacts, permissions, or evidence states stop processing.
6. **Auditable limits:** every report identifies the processing contract, model/config identity,
   human-review state, provenance, and limitations.

## Business-model hypotheses

Possible future models include a paid professional pilot, per-project analysis, an annual
professional licence, a controlled private deployment, or an enterprise contract. No price,
revenue, customer, or product-market-fit claim is made. Commercial validation requires separate,
documented customer discovery and cannot be inferred from technical or scientific results.

## Success for a future pilot

A pilot would be successful only if authorized professional users find the workflow useful and
auditable, reviewers can preserve the evidence boundary, security/privacy controls pass the
required gates, and limitations remain visible. Predictive scores alone cannot establish product
success or professional acceptability.
