# Phase 6B customer-discovery plan

## Status and purpose

This plan tests—not proves—the hypothesis that professional archaeological and heritage teams may
benefit from terrain-pattern screening that prioritizes expert review while leaving archaeological
interpretation to humans. Phase 6B is discovery infrastructure only. No interviews, customers,
pilots, willingness to pay, or product-market fit are claimed.

The initial goal is approximately **10–15 interviews with appropriate professionals** before any
major product implementation decision. Interviews should explore current work before presenting
ArchaeoAI and must not substitute for Phase 5E security, privacy, archaeological, or licensing
review.

## Evidence categories

Keep these states separate in notes and synthesis:

| State | Meaning | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PRODUCT_HYPOTHESIS` | A proposed problem, workflow, or product response. | That the problem exists or the response is useful. |
| `MARKET_HYPOTHESIS` | A proposed pattern across organizations or buyers. | Market size, prevalence, or demand. |
| `PROFESSIONAL_FEEDBACK` | Attributable or anonymized practitioner observation from a documented interaction. | Customer intent, organizational approval, or payment. |
| `CUSTOMER_INTEREST` | A relevant organization asks to continue evaluating the concept. | Pilot commitment or purchase. |
| `PILOT_INTEREST` | A person with relevant influence describes a credible path to a bounded pilot. | Approval, signed scope, data authority, or payment. |
| `PAID_WILLINGNESS` | Documented procurement/budget signal tied to a defined offer and conditions. | Revenue until an executed agreement and payment exist. |

## Interview groups

| Group | Target | Priority | Discovery purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Commercial archaeological consultancies | Primary | Understand core terrain-review workflow, repetition, professional responsibility, and pilot authority. |
| B | Heritage/archaeology teams in environmental or engineering consultancies | Primary | Understand multidisciplinary project fit, reporting, privacy, procurement, and integration. |
| C | Independent archaeological or GIS specialists | Secondary | Test technical workflow assumptions and usability for smaller teams. |
| D | Potential eventual buyers such as infrastructure or renewables heritage teams | Secondary | Understand commissioning, governance, risk, and approval—not to bypass archaeologists. |

Aim for variation in organization size, role, GIS/LiDAR use, project scale, and buying influence.
Do not claim representativeness from a small purposive sample.

## Method

1. Screen prospects with the ICP scorecard using public professional information only.
2. Invite individuals through a public professional contact route; do not scrape private details or
   mass-message.
3. Explain the student-led research purpose, voluntary nature, intended note handling, anonymity
   choice, and absence of a sales obligation.
4. Conduct a 20–30 minute semi-structured interview using the guide. Start with current workflow;
   introduce the bounded concept only after workflow, pain, and alternatives are understood.
5. Record one evidence-register entry per interaction. Separate direct statements, interviewer
   summaries, and inferences; obtain permission before using an attributable quotation.
6. Tag evidence by role, group, strength, and hypothesis; record contradictory evidence and
   non-response without converting either into support.
7. Review patterns after roughly five interviews and again at 10–15. Method changes must be dated
   and cannot rewrite earlier evidence.
8. Produce a decision memo stating what is supported, contradicted, still unknown, and what action
   is justified. Discovery alone cannot authorize a pilot.

## Evidence quality

Confidence should reflect proximity and specificity:

- **High:** first-hand recent workflow evidence with concrete examples, decision context, and
  attributable or securely retained source notes.
- **Medium:** first-hand but general or anonymized evidence with limited examples.
- **Low:** second-hand impressions, hypotheticals, ambiguous statements, or interviewer inference.

A polite positive reaction is not customer interest. Pilot interest requires a defined use case,
appropriate internal path, and explicit follow-up. Paid willingness requires a concrete budget or
procurement signal tied to a bounded offer—not an answer to a leading price question.

## Decision signals

### Evidence that would strengthen the thesis

- Repeated first-hand accounts of substantial, recurring LiDAR/DTM review burden.
- Existing manual or fragmented workflows with consequential prioritization or audit problems.
- Independent preference for bounded prioritization with archaeologist control.
- Specific requirements that align with Phase 5 contracts, private deployment, and auditable
  reporting.
- Credible requests to evaluate a bounded pilot, including an approver and authorized project path.

### Evidence that would weaken the thesis

- Terrain review is rare, small, or peripheral for primary groups.
- Existing tools and processes are considered adequate at acceptable effort.
- Prioritization output would add review burden without changing decisions.
- Trust, liability, data handling, or integration concerns remain dominant after safeguards are
  explained.
- Interest comes mainly from people without workflow involvement or organizational influence.

### Evidence that would force redesign

- Professionals need a different unit of work, report, integration, or evidence sequence.
- Local/private deployment is unusable while SaaS risk remains unacceptable.
- Score visibility creates anchoring, or the proposed review queue conflicts with professional
  practice.
- The narrow bowl-barrow scope offers no useful workflow entry point despite a real adjacent need.

Redesign evidence permits a revised hypothesis, not automatic expansion of scientific claims or a
new model.

### Commercial NO-GO or broad pivot signals

- Consistent evidence that primary users do not perform or value this terrain-screening task.
- Existing alternatives solve it adequately and switching has little value.
- Required data rights, privacy, liability, model/IP, or professional safeguards are impractical.
- No credible pilot path emerges after the planned interviews and appropriate follow-up.
- Achievable workflow value is too small to justify procurement, support, or operational risk.

## Analysis safeguards

Report counts with denominators and participant mix; do not generalize beyond the interviewed
sample. Preserve negative cases, distinguish prompted from spontaneous comments, avoid combining
multiple roles into a fictional “average customer,” and never convert interview sentiment into
scientific model evidence. The spent external test and private candidate material remain outside
customer discovery.
