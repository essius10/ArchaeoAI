# Commercial pivot and no-go criteria

## Purpose

Phase 6B should test whether a real professional problem exists, not force the current product
hypothesis to survive. These criteria guide a later evidence-based decision; none has been observed
or triggered yet.

## Continue the current hypothesis only when

Repeated evidence from relevant primary users shows a material terrain-review workflow, current
alternatives leave an important gap, bounded prioritization fits professional practice, safeguards
are acceptable, and a credible authorized pilot path exists. Positive sentiment alone is
insufficient.

## Redesign triggers

Redesign the workflow—without expanding scientific claims—if professionals identify a real need
but consistently require a different unit of work, review sequence, explanation, report,
integration, deployment model, or evidence presentation. A redesign must become a new testable
hypothesis and preserve the Phase 5 contracts and evidence ladder unless separately reviewed.

## Commercial NO-GO triggers

- Professionals in the primary groups rarely inspect LiDAR/DTM or the workload is too small or
  infrequent to matter.
- Existing manual, GIS, automated, or commercial tools already solve the problem adequately.
- AI prioritization adds little value, increases review burden, or creates unacceptable anchoring or
  missed-feature risk.
- Liability, trust, professional-responsibility, or workflow barriers dominate after realistic
  safeguards are explained.
- Commercial source-data or derived-output rights are unavailable or impractical.
- Model ownership, distribution, runtime authority, or safe loading cannot be resolved.
- Privacy, sensitive-location, security, deletion, or candidate-publication controls cannot meet
  professional requirements.
- Relevant professionals consistently reject the human-in-the-loop workflow.
- No credible authorized pilot path appears after approximately 10–15 appropriate interviews and
  reasonable follow-up.
- Achievable value is too small to justify procurement, deployment, support, or operational risk.

## Broad pivot options

If the current hypothesis weakens, possible directions for later evaluation are:

- remain a research and reproducibility project rather than a commercial product;
- focus on professional workflow/audit tooling without model scoring;
- provide a privately integrated analytic component within an existing professional system;
- narrow the supported task or user group while retaining human authority;
- pause commercial work until licensing, evidence, or security conditions change.

These are categories, not new startup proposals or authorized phases. Any pivot involving a new
model, class, geography, dataset, interface, or deployment requires its own scientific and product
gates.

## Decision discipline

At the planned synthesis point, report evidence for and against each hypothesis, participant mix,
confidence, missing perspectives, and unresolved Phase 6A blockers. Record `CONTINUE TESTING`,
`REDESIGN AND RETEST`, `PAUSE`, or `COMMERCIAL NO-GO` with rationale. Do not reinterpret weak
evidence, change the interview sample solely to find support, or convert research performance into
commercial validation.
