## Prospective feasibility, precision and power

The following numbers are **hypothetical design calculations**.

- At illustrative prevalence 0.1%, 100,000 probability-sampled units yield 100 expected targets
  before missingness. At 0.01%, one million units are needed for that expectation. Neither
  expectation guarantees a usable count. A selected list of recorded sites can support only
  known-feature recall and requires its own coverage/reference definition.
- A worst-case binomial 95% half-width of 0.05 needs about 385 independent adjudications for
  a proportion. Equal clusters of 20 and ICC 0.05 give a planning design effect of
  1 + 19 × 0.05 = 1.95, or roughly 751 adjudications before weights, missingness, and rounding
  to complete blocks. Unequal weights and spatial clustering can increase this substantially.
- For paired block differences in useful-prioritization rate, hypothetical improvement 0.10,
  SD of paired differences 0.20, two-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power yield
  ceil(((1.96 + 0.84) × 0.20 / 0.10)²) = 32 independent paired blocks as a normal approximation.
  Under SD 0.35 the same calculation gives 97 blocks. Confirm using a design-specific simulation
  with feasible geography, reviewer effects, rare positives, missingness and a small-sample
  analysis before adoption. These values cannot establish power in the current project.
- Zero false alerts in independent verified non-target units would require roughly 2,995 such
  units to bound the false-alert probability below 0.1% with a one-sided 95% exact interval.
  This is an idealized benchmark, with no independence claim for terrain windows.
- At five minutes each, 751 first reviews cost about 63 reviewer-hours, or 125 hours for
  two full reads, before adjudication, reference research and overhead. Currency cost is
  approved hours × the actual rate plus data, preparation and adjudication costs. No rates,
  reviewer capacity or funding are verified. Calculator compute is negligible. An empirical run
  needs a timed synthetic throughput check and a cost/coverage inventory before commitment.

Before score access, require a rights-checked area frame with independent blocks excluded from
E001, external evaluation and prior private inference. Test independence against private footprints
under separate custodian approval. Record acquisition lineage, coverage and excluded area,
processing/ground-cover strata and acquisition-vs-geography imbalance. Prespecify a deduplication
rule, block-size sensitivity analysis for scale/MAUP, baseline ordering, review budget, adjudication,
unknown-label bounds and missingness reporting. If a reviewer samples outputs with unequal
probability, retain inclusion probabilities and denominators. Require overlap across relevant
strata and a precision gate before any population estimate. An archaeology reference source and
unit-to-object matching rule are prerequisites for false-alert and known-feature recall claims.

## Five-minute student walkthrough

Run `python scripts/rare_target_scenarios.py` to see the hypothetical table. With 100,000
decisions, prevalence 0.1%, sensitivity 90% and specificity 99%, expect 90 true target alerts and
999 false alerts. Precision is 90/1,089 (~8.3%) despite 94.5% balanced accuracy. At two minutes
per alert the expected workload is 2,178 minutes. Halving that mean is different from guaranteeing
a fixed budget. The calculator multiplies conditional rates by the hypothetical base rates, then
divides true alerts by all alerts. Its inputs are assumptions. E001 background performance never
enters the calculator. Ask yourself why case-control BA cannot supply field prevalence, why
overlapping windows cannot be counted as independent archaeological objects, and what evidence
would let you compare useful decisions per hour across reviewers. Student understanding has yet
to be assessed.
